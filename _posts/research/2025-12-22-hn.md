## Introduction

In my [previous post](https://bhqpham.com/research/2025/12/15/ebm.html), I provided a quick introduction to Energy-based modeling (**EBM**), which is an alternative approach to [maximum likelihood estimation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_likelihood_estimation). Obviously, much of the concepts in EBM are borrowed from Physics (specifically, thermodynamics). Thus, it is not a surprise that early attempts of generative modeling or machine learning also came from physicists and pure mathematicians. 


In this post, I am keen to introduce the concept of the Classical Associative Memory (AM) systems, also commonly known as [Hopfield networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopfield_network). Such systems were first introduced by [Shun'ichi Amari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shun%27ichi_Amari) in **1972**, and rediscovered and popularized by [John J. Hopfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Hopfield) in **1982**. In recent times, both individuals have finally received big accolades for their achievements. For examples, Amari received the Kyoto Prize in 2025 and Hopfield received the Nobel Prize in Physics in 2024. I would like to stress that the introduction of AM systems truly had helped pave the way for more advance generative models, e.g., [Boltzmann Machines](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~fritz/absps/cogscibm.pdf) and even [diffusion models](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.03585) (which were also introduced by physicists). 

## Recurrency via Energy Minimization

Hopfield networks were the earliest recurrent neural networks and working examples of generative models. One can generate new patterns[^1], and even perform pattern recognition or recovery. For example, you can provide a perturbed example and the model can effectively ***denoise*** or ***fill in*** the missing blanks of the pattern.


[^1]: These new patterns are called **spurious patterns**.
